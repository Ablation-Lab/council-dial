# Council Dial Lite runs on the sample, 8 November 2026

This file scores the first four real runs of [Council Dial Lite](https://github.com/Ablation-Lab/council-dial-lite) on this repository's sample (`samples/target/`), three of Lite 1.0.0 on Sonnet (sections 1 to 6) and one of Lite 1.1.0 on Opus (section 7), against [`sample-answers/expected-findings.md`](../sample-answers/expected-findings.md), and compares them with the two full-edition runs in [`20261107-council-runs.md`](20261107-council-runs.md). Findings are cited with their run id, as there: `lite-20261108-b41d/judge/3` is finding 3 in that run's judge's final ranked list, and `20261107-2f23/M1` is the full edition's merged finding M1.

**Read this first.** These are six single runs, in four different setups, and they can't show that any setup is better:
- **Different models.** The three Lite 1.0.0 runs asked for Sonnet for every subagent. The Lite 1.1.0 run and the full-edition runs asked for no model, so their subagents inherited the main session's model (Opus 5.5); Claude Code doesn't report the model a subagent actually ran on.
- **Different material for one run.** Full-edition run `20261107-f675` ran on an older version of the sample, whose README said it had planted problems and didn't say the file name comes from user input, with a target description that also mentioned planted problems. Only `20261107-2f23` and the Lite runs reviewed the current, neutral sample.
- **The sample is small and easy:** 30 lines of code and a 7-line README.

### Severity

The answer key gives no severities, so this uses a scale of our own: HIGH for a security breach, data loss, or the main sign-up flow failing for a whole class of ordinary input (#1, #7, #8, and #5 as part of #1); MED for a README promise broken on ordinary input (#2, #3, #4, #5 on its own); LOW for edge cases (#6, #9). Other scales are reasonable, so read this as one view.

| | Lite H | Lite A | Lite M | Full 2 | Full 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Planted problems at that severity | 7 of 9 | 8 of 9 | 9 of 9 | 9 of 9 | 8 of 8 kept |
| Differs | #7 MED (low), #9 MED (high) | #4 LOW (low) | none | none | none |

**The judges' downgrades.** The five judges marked 16 findings OVERSTATED with a lower severity than a reviewer gave (2 in Lite H, 5 in Lite A, 2 in Lite M, 5 in Full 2, 2 in Full 1). We'd call 14 right and 2 wrong:
- **Wrong:** Lite H's judge lowered #7, the path from user input, from HIGH to MED, although the README says the name is what the user typed. Lite A's judge lowered #4 from MED to LOW, calling the mismatch trivial; it breaks a documented promise on ordinary input, though only cosmetically, so this one is arguable.
- **Right:** all five lowered #6 (atomic save) to LOW, and Lite A, Full 2 and Full 1 lowered #9 (`['']`) to LOW: both need a crash, concurrent writers or an empty list. So do Lite A's `g`-flag cut (HIGH to MED), the cuts of caller-supplied `maxLength` problems (Lite A, Full 2) and of reserved-word findings (Lite M, Full 2), and Full 2's cut of the load-and-save race (HIGH to MED).

## 4. Run-to-run variation

Lite A and Lite M had the same setup: the same Lite files, model request, material, target description and folder, about 15 minutes apart. They still differed:

| | Lite A | Lite M |
|---|---|---|
| Reviewers' findings (correctness, security, docs) | 26 (12, 7, 7) | 21 (10, 5, 6) |
| Merged by the judge | 16 | 14 |
| Kept: REAL, OVERSTATED | 13: 7, 6 | 14: 11, 3 |
| Dismissed | 3 | 0 |
| Kept by severity (HIGH, MED, LOW) | 3, 2, 8 | 3, 4, 7 |

Most of the difference in dismissals came from the reviewers, not the judge: none of Lite M's reviewers raised two of the three findings Lite A's judge dismissed (`İ`, and the header "conflict"). The third, unbounded input, Lite M's security reviewer did raise (its finding 4), and Lite M's judge kept it inside judge/9 and judge/12, where Lite A's judge dismissed it as covered by another finding. Where the same finding came up, the judges drew the line in different places: reserved slugs were OVERSTATED in Lite A and Lite M, but NOT REAL in Lite H. The judges also split the OVERSTATED label very differently (6 of 13 kept findings in Lite A, 3 of 14 in Lite M).

What stayed stable: all 9 planted problems kept, and the three HIGH findings (#1, #7, #8) at HIGH in both. With one run per setup, a difference between two setups smaller than this spread says nothing.

## 6. What the runs showed about Lite itself

- **Copying.** The check found every finding in all three summaries copied exactly: 9, 13 and 14 MATCHES, with no CHANGED, NO SOURCE or LEFT OUT.
- **Saving.** In Lite M, two of the three reviewers replied without saving their reports: they never called the Write tool. In Lite H, four agents first tried to save with shell commands, which were refused. Lite 1.1.0 puts the save line first and again last in each prompt.
- **Saved files against replies.** In Lite H, two saved files differed from the replies: the correctness reviewer's dropped its formatting, and the judge's was worded differently in places. In the interactive runs, the only differences were whitespace at the very end, which Lite 1.1.0 says doesn't count.
- **Permission prompts.** In Lite M, in Manual mode with the `Edit(/reviews/lite/**)` rule, the only prompts were 8, for the main session's reads of the brief templates. Lite A, in auto mode, shows nothing about prompts.
- **Rules the main session skipped or found unclear** in Lite M, all addressed in Lite 1.1.0: the check's prompt order, comparing each brief with its templates as a step of its own, logging what it did differently, the run id's date and random characters, `savedMatchesReply` for a report Claude saved, the appeal's entry when it doesn't run, the summary's headings, where a finding ends, citing a judge's merged list labelled with letters, the critical path with a baseline, headings an agent adds to its report, whether the check gets the material lines, and estimates from fewer than 5 runs.

## 7. Lite 1.1.0 on Opus: `lite-20261108-01`

Lite O ran in the same scratch folder as Lite A and Lite M, with the same description of the target, interactively in Manual mode with the `Edit(/reviews/lite/**)` rule, and with Lite 1.1.0 loaded with `--plugin-dir`. No model was set, so its subagents inherited the main session's model (Opus 5.5), as in both full-edition runs. That makes it the fairest comparison with them, though Full 2's description of the target was worded differently, and Full 1 reviewed the older sample.

**Two things changed at once.** Lite O is the first run of Lite 1.1.0, whose save line comes first and again last, and the first Lite run on Opus. All six of its agents saved their own reports, but the reviewers who skipped the save in Lite M were on Sonnet, so this run can't show which change, if either, made the difference.

- **Findings:** on the same model, Lite O's council kept as many as Full 2's: all 9 planted problems and 12 findings, with the same HIGH, MED and LOW counts. The details differ: Lite O put #4 at LOW and #5 at MED where Full 2 had MED and HIGH (8 of 9 on our scale, against 9 of 9), Full 2 kept reserved names and the load-and-save race as findings of their own, and the judges split REAL and OVERSTATED differently (10 and 2, against 7 and 5).

### The appeal so far

The four runs with an appeal are Lite H, Lite A, Lite O and Full 2. Between them, the appeal checked 8 dismissals and called all 8 fair. It took 15.1, 20.7, 18.6 and 8.2 seconds, and 29,500 to 43,947 tokens. The only dismissal we'd call wrong so far, Full 1's dismissal of the path bug (wrong by today's answer key; see section 3), was in Full 1, the one run whose type had no appeal. So the appeal hasn't yet met a wrong dismissal, and these runs say nothing about whether it would catch one.

### What it showed about Lite 1.1.0

- **Template reads:** one prompt. The first read was `check-brief.md`, and choosing "Yes, allow reading from …/skills/council-dial-lite during this session" covered the other 4 reads. The session read only the 5 files a Standard council needs, each by name.
- **Saving:** all six agents saved their own reports with the Write tool. Each saved file matched the agent's reply apart from whitespace at the very end, which Lite 1.1.0 doesn't count.
- **Prompts:** every agent's prompt had the save line first and the reminder last, with absolute paths. The check got no material lines, and its file list named both the judge's and the appeal's reports.
- **The run id** was `lite-20261108-01`, found with a Glob and then confirmed by reading its exact path.
- **The check:** 12 of 12 findings MATCHES, with no CHANGED, NO SOURCE or LEFT OUT.
- **Deviations:** none logged. It followed two rules in spirit rather than to the letter, and Lite 1.1.1 now clarifies both:
  - It didn't read the security reviewer's report again before starting the judge, or the appeal's report before the check, because it had compared each one after the last running agent finished.
  - The run-id rule didn't say whether ids ending in 4 hex characters count as numbers. It rightly didn't count them.
